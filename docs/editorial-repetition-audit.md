# Editorial repetition audit

## Scope

Reviewed the 25 published STHENO Insights guides and the shared rendering architecture. The exercise library is outside this editorial review.

## Finding

The corpus has strong practical language but a conspicuous shared footprint: every guide uses the same introduction extension, section order, callout, table, four-week workbook, safety paragraph, bottom line, and CTA. Pillar-specific copy changes the substance, but the repeated frame makes unrelated topics feel machine-produced.

## Highest-priority corrections

1. Replace the single universal article renderer with four intentional formats: practical guide, evidence explainer, myth investigation, and decision comparison.
2. Keep direct-answer openings for query-driven guides; preserve narrative openings where the title promises a personal or situational story.
3. Remove sections that do not earn their place. A supplement explainer does not need the same workbook as a schedule guide.
4. Give each article claim-level citations rather than repeating two pillar-level sources indefinitely.
5. Vary conclusions: a decision rule, checklist, worked example, or limitations section may close the piece more naturally than “The bottom line.”

## Current release decision

KEEP all 25 URLs indexed because each addresses a distinct reader question and contains substantive topic-specific guidance. Do not pad for word count. The present sprint corrects authorship, dates, source links, schema, metadata, and transparency. Full prose restructuring remains an editorial rewrite and must be performed article by article so the existing human voice is not replaced with another template.

## Repeated elements to retire selectively

- “The useful question is not what looks most impressive on paper…”
- “Use this as a starting experiment rather than a permanent rule.”
- Identical “STHENO principle” callout
- Identical measurement caveat
- Identical four-week protocol
- Identical safety paragraph on articles that do not make symptom-related claims
- Identical final consistency paragraph

## Quality gate for future articles

Before publication, confirm that the opening answers the reader’s actual intent, headings reflect the topic rather than a house template, factual claims have verified sources, dates are truthful, and the piece remains useful when read without the CTA.
